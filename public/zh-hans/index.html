Some of our number is too high. A company that over-hired can say “AI” when it means “costs.” That is AI washing. It is why every event carries a confidence tier, and why “confirmed” only means the company said so.

Some of it is too low. The cheapest cut is the job that is never posted. No press release, no layoff, nothing for a tracker to catch. That shows up in hiring data, not layoff data. It is the next section.

Part 2

Why the official numbers look calm

If the cuts are real, why is unemployment 4.1%? Because of what that number leaves out.

“But unemployment is only 4.1%”

True. It is also the number least able to see this. The headline rate counts people who looked for work in the last four weeks and found none. Walk down from it.

The job that was never posted. When a role is automated through attrition, nobody is laid off. Someone leaves and the opening is never listed. Headcount shrinks, the work is gone, and the unemployment rate does not move. It lands on the people who would have been hired: the third rung above.

“The easiest people to fire are the people you have not hired yet.” Andrew Yang, Jubilee Surrounded, September 2026

The immigration factor. A smaller labor pool also holds the unemployment rate down while hiring slows. Fewer people looking means a lower rate by definition.

那新增工作岗位在哪里？

没人说出口的裁决： We’ve been in a white-collar recession since the pandemic — with a brief reprieve in 2022. In 2025, if you strip out healthcare, the U.S. economy net lost jobs. That’s the definition of a recession, just not one any official body called. In 2026, we’re actively shedding jobs — and now it’s visible even in the headline numbers. The AI displacement didn’t start this. But it turned a slow leak into a flood.

The U.S. added 116,000 net new jobs in all of 2025 in current BLS data (BLS payroll survey, Dec 2024 to Dec 2025). Health care alone added 387,000, and 686,000 with social assistance. Strip out healthcare, and the rest of the American economy net lost jobs. So when someone says “AI will create new jobs” — we actually know what those jobs are. They’re nursing jobs.

Two economies

Whatever happens to the job count, the gains are already landing in one place.

Read it as a K. The job-market measures average 12. Those are the ones the headlines watch, and they are calm. The measures of who gets the gains average 100. The job market is the calm half of this index. It is also the half that the cuts on this page push on.
Where is the breaking point? Nobody knows. 100 would mean all six at their worst at the same moment, which has never happened. The index reached 71 in Nov 2009 and 80 in Apr 2020. Both times the job market recovered and the index fell. The wealth and wage measures are at their extremes anyway. For a sense of what breaking has looked like: in 1933, about 25% of American workers were unemployed (Lebergott estimate, in Margo, Journal of Economic Perspectives, 1933). The summer before, the Army cleared jobless veterans out of Washington.

The Second Wave

We document what companies announce. Nobody tracks what happens next — and what happens next is larger.

THIS ALREADY HAPPENED. IN SLOW MOTION.

When manufacturing left Ohio and Michigan in the 1980s, it took 20 years for the full damage to show. The steel mill closed, then the diner across from it, then the school tax base, then the hospital wing. Economists called it job multiplier collapse. 4.7 million manufacturing jobs vanished — and dragged an estimated 7–8 million more with them.

AI displacement is the same mechanism. Compressed into years, not decades. And it’s not hitting Youngstown — it’s hitting San Francisco, New York, Seattle, Austin, and Boston simultaneously.

The barista problem: The 547,016 jobs in this tracker are the ones whose companies issued press releases. For each of them, there are service workers — the lunch spots, dry cleaners, gyms, and Ubers near the office — whose customer base quietly vanished. Those jobs don’t get a press release. They get a lease non-renewal.
The geography trap: Manufacturing displacement was regionally contained — you could point to Youngstown, Gary, Detroit. AI displacement is concentrated in the cities with the highest cost of living: SF, NYC, Seattle, Austin, Boston. The places that grew fastest on knowledge-worker salaries are now losing the income that made their housing math work. The real estate correction follows the labor correction. It already started.

Part 5

The best case against us

A page like this should be argued with. These are the strongest replies we know.

The best objections

Each of these is partly right. If we cannot answer them, you should not believe the rest of this page. Open any one to read our reply.

“AI automates tasks, not jobs. A job is a bundle of tasks.”
What is trueAlmost no job can be handed over whole. People keep the parts that need judgment and trust.
What it missesA team of 100 that loses 60% of its tasks does not keep 100 people on the remaining 40%. The job survives. Most of the jobs do not.
“Technology has always created more jobs than it destroyed.”
What is trueIt has. About four in ten American workers farmed in 1900. Fewer than two in a hundred do now, and the country is richer for it.
What it missesThat shift took generations, and it was not gentle while it happened. This one is measured in years, and it is the first to do the thinking work that people moved into last time.
“Cheaper means more demand. Look at radiologists.”
What is trueWhere people want far more of something than they can afford, lower cost grows the field. Radiology is hiring.
What it missesIt only works where demand is nowhere near full. Nobody files more insurance claims because claims got cheaper to process.
“The World Economic Forum expects 170 million new jobs against 92 million displaced.”
What is trueThat is the forecast: worldwide, from every cause and not AI alone, by 2030. World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs 2025, by 2030
What it missesA net figure hides who. The new jobs are not offered to the people displaced, in the place they live, at the pay they had. And an American salary is among the most expensive to keep.
“New business formation is booming. People are becoming founders.”
What is trueApplications are running at about 500,000 a month, well above 2019. Census Business Formation Statistics, Oct 2026
What it missesBusinesses that hire are growing about half as fast: the Census Bureau expects 5.6% of applications to become employers within a year, down from 6.9% in 2019. A laid-off analyst who registers a consulting company counts as a new business.
“Companies are already rehiring. Look at Klarna.”
What is trueKlarna did walk it back. In May 2025 its chief executive said the cost focus had lowered quality, and the company began hiring people for customer service again. Entrepreneur, reporting a Bloomberg interview, May 2025
What it missesA reversal belongs on the record next to the cut, and we log the ones we find. Rehiring some of a team is not rehiring the team.
“Half of these ‘AI layoffs’ are ordinary cost cuts with a better story.”
What is trueSome are. That is AI washing, and it is why only part of our count is marked confirmed.
What it missesIt cuts the other way too: most displacement never becomes a layoff at all. That is why the stronger evidence on this page is about hiring, not firing.
